Rubbish Removal Launceston: Skip Bins vs Pick-up

Your options for rubbish removal in Launceston

In short: For a big clean-up, a skip bin is usually the simplest way to remove household or garden waste. For a load or two, the Launceston Waste Centre may be enough, and the City's yearly hard waste collection takes some bulky items.

Most Launceston households have three ways to get rid of more rubbish than fits in their bins:

  1. A skip bin, delivered to your driveway and taken away when you're done.
  2. Taking it to the tip yourself, at the City of Launceston's Launceston Waste Centre in Mowbray.
  3. The annual hard waste collection, a once-a-year, pre-registered kerbside pick-up.

Which one makes sense depends on how much you have, what it is, and whether you have a trailer and the time to do the runs.

Option 1: a skip bin

In short: A skip suits jobs with a lot of mixed rubbish, heavy waste or many trailer loads of garden waste, because you load it at your own pace and it's taken away in one go.

A skip sits on your driveway (or on the street with council approval) for an agreed hire period. You can fill it over a few days, and you don't need a trailer, a ute or anyone to lift heavy items. Skips can take things the hard waste collection won't, such as green waste, mattresses, bricks, rubble and bagged household rubbish, as long as you choose the right bin and tell the operator. They can't take asbestos, gas bottles, batteries, tyres or chemicals in a normal load. See what can go in a skip and the size guide.

For a deceased estate, a house move or a big shed or garage job, see our clean-outs guide.

Option 2: the Launceston Waste Centre

In short: If you have a trailer and only a load or two, you can take rubbish to the Launceston Waste Centre yourself. Recyclable items are free in domestic quantities, and your yearly entry voucher covers one visit.

The City of Launceston's Launceston Waste Centre and Transfer Station is on Cavalry Road, Mowbray. The City lists it as open 7:30am to 4:15pm, 7 days a week, closed on Good Friday and Christmas Day. Domestic quantities of permitted recyclables are free at its Recycling Centre, including furniture, household appliances, scrap metal, e-waste, cardboard, paint, gas bottles and batteries. General waste, garden organics, mattresses, clean fill and tyres at the transfer station are charged. Next door, the Uptipity second-hand shop sells items recovered from the waste stream.

The City also gives each household one voucher a year for a single free entry to a Council-managed waste centre to dispose of domestic items. The smaller Lilydale Transfer Station opens on Sunday mornings.

Option 3: the annual hard waste collection

In short: The City runs one hard waste collection a year, usually in March. You must register beforehand, a registration charge applies, and it won't take green waste, mattresses or rubble.

The City of Launceston's hard waste collection is an annual chance to put out items that can't go in your bins, such as whitegoods, furniture and old TVs. The 2026 collection ran from 16 to 27 March, and the City says registrations usually open in February. Only properties that have pre-registered are collected.

  • Limits: up to 2 m³ per property, each item must be safe for two people to lift and under 1.9 m long, and doors must be removed from fridges, freezers and washing machines.
  • It won't take: asbestos or other hazardous waste, tyres, gas bottles, loose rubble, bricks, concrete, green waste, food waste, mattresses, liquids, paint, batteries and broken glass.

If you miss the registration period, the City says you can take your items to the Launceston Waste Centre.

What about my kerbside bins?

In short: Your bins are for everyday rubbish and recycling, not clean-ups.

The City collects a red or pink-lid general waste bin weekly and a yellow-lid recycling bin fortnightly. A green-lid FOGO bin for food and garden organics is available by registration and is collected fortnightly. Heavy builders' rubble, large timber, concrete, bricks and asbestos can't go in your general waste bin.

So which is easier?

In short: Pick a skip for big, mixed or heavy jobs. Use the waste centre or the yearly hard waste collection for small amounts of what they accept.

  • A skip bin usually makes sense for a full clean-out, a renovation, a big garden or shed job, heavy waste, or when you don't have a trailer or the time for several tip runs.
  • The Launceston Waste Centre suits one or two trailer loads, especially recyclables, which are free in domestic quantities.
  • The hard waste collection suits furniture and whitegoods, if you've registered in time.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest way to get rid of a lot of rubbish in Launceston?

It depends on how much you have and what it is. For one or two loads, a trailer run to the Launceston Waste Centre in Mowbray, using your yearly free entry voucher, may be enough. For a big clean-out, heavy waste or many loads, a skip bin is often the easier option, because you load it at your own pace and it's taken away in one trip.

Where is the Launceston tip and when is it open?

The City of Launceston's Launceston Waste Centre is on Cavalry Road, Mowbray. The City lists it as open 7:30am to 4:15pm, 7 days a week, closed on Good Friday and Christmas Day.

When is the Launceston hard waste collection?

It's an annual collection, historically held in March with registrations open in February. The 2026 collection ran from 16 to 27 March. You must register beforehand, and a registration charge applies.

What won't the Launceston hard waste collection take?

Asbestos and other hazardous waste, tyres, gas bottles, loose rubble, bricks, concrete, green waste, food waste, mattresses, liquids, paint, batteries and broken glass. Items must be liftable by two people, under 1.9 m long, and the total can't exceed 2 m³.

Do I get a free tip voucher in Launceston?

The City says each household gets one voucher a year for a single free entry to a Council-managed waste centre to dispose of domestic items.

Can a skip bin take garden waste and building rubbish?

Usually, yes, which is one reason people choose a skip. Heavy waste such as soil, concrete and bricks normally needs a smaller bin with a weight limit, and asbestos can never go in a general skip. Tell the operator what you have when you request a quote.

Do I need a permit for a skip bin on the street in Launceston?

A skip wholly on your own property generally doesn't need council approval. A skip on the road, footpath or verge usually needs approval from the City of Launceston. See our council skip permits guide for details.
