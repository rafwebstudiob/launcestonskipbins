Skip bin permits in Launceston: City of Launceston rules

In short: What City of Launceston requires, how to apply, and where to place a skip on your own property.

A skip on your own driveway usually doesn't need council approval. A skip on the road, footpath or nature strip is different. Here's what City of Launceston says and what to check before you book.

Last updated: 24 October 2026

The short answer

On your own land, generally no. On a road, footpath or nature strip, yes: the City of Launceston requires an Occupation of Roads, Parks and Public Reserves Permit, applied for at least 10 working days ahead. The skip company usually applies.

On your own property

Your own driveway, yard or a firm patch of ground is the easiest place for a skip. Council's permit is for skips on a road, footpath or other public land, so a bin wholly on your property generally doesn't need it. On steep blocks, check the truck can safely reverse in and that the bin sits level.

On the road, footpath or nature strip

The City of Launceston requires an Occupation of Roads, Parks and Public Reserves Permit for a skip bin on a road, footpath or nature strip. Council asks for applications at least 10 working days ahead, the skip can't be placed until the permit is issued, and the permit must be fixed where it can be seen on the bin. Fees apply, reflective markers are needed if the bin stays out overnight, and a skip in a metered parking bay adds bay-hooding fees. Council can remove bins placed without a permit.

The applicant needs $20 million public liability insurance and may need a traffic management plan, so the skip company usually applies. Before you book a skip on the street, check with the operator and the City of Launceston on 03 6323 3000 or contactus@launceston.tas.gov.au, or visit the council website. Mention it on the quote form too.

What to ask council before you book

  1. Does my spot need an Occupation of Roads, Parks and Public Reserves Permit, and will the skip company apply for it?
  2. What does it cost this financial year, and how long does assessment take?
  3. How long can the skip stay, and are there conditions such as lights, reflectors or a set distance from the kerb?
  4. Are there extra rules for a busy road, a bus stop or a heritage area?

Ask for the answers in writing or note the date and who you spoke to.

Placing the skip safely

Keep the skip clear of driveways, hydrants, bus stops and drains, and don't fill above the rim. If it stays on the road overnight, it needs reflective markers. Given the permit lead time and insurance requirement, it's usually easier to find room on your own property.
